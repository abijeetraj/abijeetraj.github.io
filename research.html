Research Themes

  • Uncertainty in rollouts of learned time-stepping operators

    Aug 2026–present
    Johns Hopkins University Baltimore, MD

    PI: Dr. Somdatta Goswami

    Studying how uncertainty propagates through autoregressive rollouts of learned time-stepping neural operators (DeepONet family), where errors compound as each prediction becomes the next input. The direction I am proposing is to separate per-step model error from the system's chaotic amplification, and to use the resulting uncertainty to weight ensemble Kalman filter updates in place of hand-tuned inflation, on benchmarks such as Lorenz-96, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky and 2D Kolmogorov flow.

    Ongoing.

  • GPU acceleration of a differentiable PDE solver

    Aug 2026–present
    Johns Hopkins University Baltimore, MD

    PI: Dr. Somdatta Goswami

    Accelerating the group's differentiable finite-element PDE solver on GPUs by writing custom sparse CUDA kernels and testing multigrid preconditioners and Krylov methods, so that ensemble and Bayesian methods become affordable without large clusters.

    Ongoing.

  • Uncertainty-Aware Machine Learning and Inverse Mix Design for Engineered Cementitious Composites

    Dec 2025–June 2026
    University of Texas Rio Grande Valley Edinburg, TX

    PI: Dr. Jinwoo An

    Built a calibrated surrogate for engineered cementitious composites from sparse experimental data, replacing point predictions with distribution-free conformal intervals that carry finite-sample coverage guarantees. Decomposing the predictive variance showed that about 68% of it was epistemic, so the bottleneck was missing information rather than noise. Predictions were stress-tested with Monte Carlo simulation of input and process variability.

  • Vision-Guided Williams-Enriched Physics Informed Neural Network for Mesh-Free Stress Intensity Factor Assessment of Cracked Concrete

    Dec 2025–June 2026
    University of Texas Rio Grande Valley Edinburg, TX

    PI: Dr. Jinwoo An

    Proposed and built a pipeline that extracts crack geometry from images and passes it to a physics-informed neural network (PINN) that estimates the stress intensity factor, automating a modeling step that is otherwise done by hand. Along the way I diagnosed the PINN's failure at the crack-tip singularity (spectral bias) and replaced the residual loss with a variational objective and a J-integral read-out, which needs no tuned loss weights.

  • Cross-modal adaptive exploration and chemo-visual hazard-aware navigation for autonomous robotic inspection in hazardous environments

    May 2025–Dec 2025
    Kyungpook National University Remote

    PIs: Dr. Bubryur Kim and Dr. Yuvaraj Natarajan

    As the project's first member and lead, designed the perception module for a rover that maps fire, smoke and chemical hazards: a real-time object detector adapted from a single-stage architecture, reaching mAP@0.5 of 0.845 across six hazard classes with 24.3 ms inference on a Jetson Orin Nano. Vision was fused with chemical sensors through a Kalman filter whose noise levels had to be set by hand, because the perception model gave no calibrated uncertainty. That problem is what led me to uncertainty quantification.
